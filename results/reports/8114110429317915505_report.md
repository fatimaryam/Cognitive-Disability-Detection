## Clinical Findings
Quantitative analysis demonstrated modest quantitative variation across the three drawing tasks. While the clock drawing contributed significantly, highlighting higher localized atypical regions, the trail making evidenced considerable atypicality, albeit surpassing both the clock and copy drawing tasks. These observations suggest the need for holistic interpretation rather than isolated assessment of individual components.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 96.9% for Normal cognition and 3.1% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation
Routine clinical follow-up may be considered should cognitive concerns arise. Given the current findings, ongoing observation could help monitor cognitive changes over time.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
