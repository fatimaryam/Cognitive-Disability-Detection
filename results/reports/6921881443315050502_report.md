## Cognitive Drawing Assessment
Assessment of the cognitive drawings revealed substantial contributions from the Clock Drawing (73.8%) followed closely by Trail Making (20.9%), while Copy Drawing contributed minimally (5.3%). Among the localized atypical regions, the highest incidence was noted in Trail Making (22.5%), significantly greater than that seen in both Clock Drawing (3.2%) and Copy Drawing (4.3%).

## Clinical Findings
Across the submitted drawings, there exists notable variability in contributions, with the Clock Drawing representing the dominant proportion. Although some localized irregularities were present—particularly pronounced in Trail Making—the overall quantitative differences suggest subtle cognitive nuances rather than clear impairments.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 93.6% for Normal cognition and 6.4% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation
Clinical reassessment can be undertaken if future concerns about cognitive function emerge. Routine clinical follow-up remains advisable given the current findings.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
