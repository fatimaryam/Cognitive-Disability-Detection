## Cognitive Drawing Assessment
The integrated drawing assessment revealed that the clock drawing contributed the highest proportion (61.8%) followed by trail making (31.6%), and then the copy drawing (6.6%). Notably, the trail making showed the largest localized atypical region affecting 40% of its area compared to smaller proportions in both the clock drawing (3.5%) and the copy drawing (4.1%).

## Clinical Findings
Across the submitted drawings, the quantitative patterns displayed modest variation but did not consistently align with typical performance profiles suggesting mild cognitive impairment. The trail making task notably exhibited higher localized atypical regions while the other drawings had relatively lower proportions of unusual areas.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 94.6% for Normal cognition and 5.4% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation
Ongoing observation may assist in monitoring cognitive status over time should further concerns arise regarding the patient’s cognitive function.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
