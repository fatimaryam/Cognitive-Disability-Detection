## Cognitive Drawing Assessment

Assessment of the cognitive drawings revealed that the Clock Drawing represented the highest contribution (72.2%) compared to the Copy Drawing (14.3%) and Trail Making (13.5%). Additionally, the Clock Drawing also showed the largest localized atypical region (9.2%), which was significantly larger than the localized atypical regions noted in both the Copy Drawing (3.8%) and Trail Making (3.8%).

## Clinical Findings

Comparison of the quantitative observations demonstrated a marked difference in contributions and localized atypical regions across the three drawing tasks. While there was notable variability in how much each drawing contributed to the overall assessment, the localized atypical regions were uniformly small, suggesting minimal spatial deviations in performance across the tasks.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 95.8% for Normal cognition and 4.2% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation

Ongoing observation may assist in monitoring cognitive status over time should any further cognitive concerns arise.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
