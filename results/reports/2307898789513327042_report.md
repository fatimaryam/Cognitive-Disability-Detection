## Clinical Findings

Evaluation of the three drawing tasks demonstrated modest quantitative variation, highlighting the Clock Drawing’s prominent role due to its largest localized atypical region (4.2%). These observations suggest heterogeneity in cognitive function but require integration with additional clinical evaluations before definitive conclusions about specific impairments can be drawn.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 40.5% for Normal cognition and 59.5% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation

Further assessment may be appropriate if cognitive symptoms progress. Routine clinical follow-up can help monitor changes in cognitive functions.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
