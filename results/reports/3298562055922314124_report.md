## Cognitive Drawing Assessment

Quantitative analysis demonstrated that the trail making test represented the highest contribution (37.2%) compared to the clock drawing (47.2%) and the copy drawing (15.5%). Each drawing task also revealed localized atypical regions, with notable variability; both the clock drawing and the copy drawing exhibited areas of localized atypicality accounting for 7.2% each, while the trail making task had slightly lower but significant localized atypicality at 3.9%.

## Clinical Findings

Assessment of the drawing tasks reflected modest quantitative variation, with no single task dominating the overall assessment. While there was considerable contribution from the clock drawing and a relatively higher presence of localized atypical regions, none of the tests individually met criteria indicative of pronounced cognitive deviation. These observations underscore the need for interpreting the drawing tasks collectively rather than isolating them for independent clinical significance.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 76.2% for Normal cognition and 23.8% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation

Routine clinical follow-up may be considered should cognitive concerns arise. Further assessment can be pursued if necessary, particularly given the nuanced findings from the integrated evaluation.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
