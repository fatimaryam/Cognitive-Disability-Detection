## Cognitive Drawing Assessment

The integrated drawing assessment demonstrated that the clock drawing significantly contributed 77.5% to the overall cognitive evaluation, followed by the trail making drawing (13.4%) and then the copy drawing (9.1%). Notably, both the clock drawing and the trail making drawing exhibited localized atypical regions, with contributions of 33.1% and 28.3%, respectively, compared to just 2.3% in the copy drawing. 

## Clinical Findings

Comparison of the quantitative observations highlighted a notable difference in localized atypical regions, particularly prominent in the clock drawing with 33.1%. This finding, along with variable contributions from the other drawing tasks, demonstrates modest quantitative variation and underscores the importance of interpreting these findings collectively rather than isolating them individually.

## Clinical Impression
The overall drawing pattern is not suggestive of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). The combined assessment demonstrated an estimated probability of 96.3% for Normal cognition and 3.7% for Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI). This interpretation is based on the integrated evaluation of all three drawing tasks rather than any individual drawing.


## Recommendation

Monitoring if cognitive concerns arise may assist in further assessing the patient’s cognitive status over time.

These findings should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical history, neurological examination, and formal cognitive assessment, and should not be considered diagnostic based solely on the drawing tasks.
